BEGIN TALK

Slide 1 - MSBA Job Matching - Yitong
Good afternoon. Our business context is graduate career services. Our primary stakeholder is a career advisor who must screen many job postings and decide which opportunities to forward, hold, or investigate for MSBA students. The problem is not simply search: the advisor must judge role fit, uncertainty, and explicit work-authorization language without losing the source evidence. Our solution is MSBA Job Matching, a grounded NLP decision-support system. It accepts a real search request or an individual posting, retrieves comparable jobs, estimates role fit, and explains the result. It is designed to make the first screen faster and more consistent while leaving forwarding, hiring, and immigration decisions with people.

Slide 2 - Separate fit, uncertainty, and risk - Yitong
The business decision has three possible actions. A strong analytics match can move forward to advisor review. A senior or engineering-heavy role should be held. Missing fields or authorization uncertainty require investigation. This distinction matters because role fit and work authorization can conflict. A posting may match SQL, Python, and dashboard skills while explicitly saying no OPT or CPT. The system therefore treats role fit, evidence completeness, and authorization language as separate signals. A high fit score never becomes proof of eligibility. For a pilot, the business measures would be high-fit precision, advisor minutes saved per posting, advisor override rate, and authorization extraction accuracy.

Slide 3 - Data volume is no longer the bottleneck - Yitong
The public source contains 785,741 real postings. We built a reproducible, stratified 100,000-row project sample, with 80,000 training rows and a fixed 20,000-row validation split. Exact-text deduplication reduced training to 77,135 unique texts. We also scanned all 785,741 original rows for explicit authorization language and found 230 unique title-level candidates. Those records are evidence candidates for human review, not verified sponsorship facts. This distinction keeps weak labels, model predictions, and genuine advisor judgments from being mixed together.

Slide 4 - What each milestone taught us - Yitong
Each milestone changed the technical decision. Milestone 2 compared static TF-IDF with contextual MiniLM representations and established the first measured baseline. Milestone 3 compared zero-shot, few-shot, and true PEFT LoRA adaptation; LoRA improved macro F1 to 87.3 percent on the weak-label benchmark. Milestone 4 integrated retrieval, classification, policy gates, and grounded output. For the final system, we added duplicate controls, full-split training, novel-text and unseen-company tests, temporal validation, calibrated review thresholds, a batch workflow, and reproducible tests. The project moved from a model comparison to an auditable advisor workflow.

Slide 5 - Final architecture - Yitong
The final pipeline has five stages. First, it routes the input as a search request or a job posting, so a search sentence is never mislabeled as a posting. Second, search mode enforces country, remote or on-site, and entry-level constraints before ranking and duplicate suppression. Third, posting mode uses a word-and-character TF-IDF LinearSVC trained on the 77,135 deduplicated rows. Fourth, class-specific margin thresholds flag uncertain cases, while missing fields and explicit authorization restrictions remain deterministic overrides. Finally, the advisor receives cited postings, the policy reason, and a recommended action. The same loaded system handles one JSON request or an entire batch CSV queue.

Handoff - Yitong to Zhike
Zhike will now show the real outputs, what worked, what did not, and how we evaluated the remaining risk.

Slide 6 - Normal search run - Zhike
This screen shows a real run for entry-level US analytics roles using SQL, Python, Excel, Tableau, or Power BI. The output exposes the parsed constraints and returns six compliant postings with title, company, country, skills, role-fit label, and evidence. The request remains in search mode rather than receiving a false posting label. We also tested six constraint combinations covering US, remote-only, on-site-only, and entry-level searches. All 36 returned results satisfied their hard constraints, and there were no duplicate company-title pairs. Authorization remains unknown unless the original posting contains explicit language.

Slide 7 - Four edge cases - Zhike
We deliberately tested more than the easiest example. Search mode returns candidates without pretending the query is a posting. A senior engineering posting is labeled low fit and held. In the most important edge case, an analytics posting receives a high role-fit prediction, but explicit No OPT or CPT text still forces review and a Hold action. A thin posting is predicted medium fit by the classifier, but missing company, location, country, and schedule fields override the displayed result to unclear. These are enforced product rules. They prevent a confident model score from hiding contradictory or incomplete evidence.

Slide 8 - More data worked; extra complexity did not - Zhike
Here is the direct worked-versus-did-not-work comparison. What did not work best was adding model complexity while using only 2,000 weakly labeled training examples. Zero-shot and few-shot prompting were useful diagnostics, and true PEFT LoRA improved macro F1 to 87.3 percent, but it did not become the best production choice. What worked was using all available deduplicated training data with combined word and character TF-IDF features. The final classifier reached 98.4 percent macro F1 on the fixed 4,000-row set and 97.9 percent on the full novel-text slice. It trains in under one minute and produces a 3.8-megabyte artifact. The lesson is task-specific: better data utilization and reproducibility mattered more here than additional model complexity.

Slide 9 - Interpret 98.4 percent carefully - Zhike
The 98.4 percent score needs an important qualification. The fixed test contains 65 errors: 3 high-fit, 13 medium-fit, 8 low-fit, and 41 unclear cases. More importantly, this benchmark measures agreement with transparent weak labels, not advisor accuracy. We replaced a uniform confidence cutoff with class-specific thresholds calibrated on 10,000 rows. On a separate 10,000-row audit, the system retains 91.63 percent of predictions at 99.47 percent weak-label accuracy and routes the remaining 8.37 percent to mandatory review. The blank human-label columns in the 1,000-row advisor queue are intentional: real advisor feedback is the next source of ground truth.

Slide 10 - Recommendation - Zhike
The completed package includes the trained classifier, calibrated review policy, hard-constraint search audit, batch CSV workflow, governance casebook, time-based validation, full-source authorization audit, 430-row evidence benchmark, and 1,000-row advisor queue. Our recommendation is a small Career Center pilot in which advisors review suggestions and record time, overrides, and error types. The system is ready to support and document that review; it is not ready to reject opportunities, forward jobs automatically, or make eligibility decisions. The business value comes from faster, more consistent first-pass prioritization, and the governance boundary is part of the product, not an afterthought. Thank you.

Q&A preparation

Q: Why not train on all 785,741 rows?
A: We scanned all 785,741 rows and packaged the authorization candidates, but training on every weak label would mainly strengthen agreement with the labeling rules. The included 1,000-row queue targets the lower-margin and authorization-sensitive cases that are most useful for human review.

Q: Why use a linear model after LoRA?
A: The LoRA result is real and remains an important adaptation experiment. The final word-and-character model can use the full training split cheaply, improves macro F1 from 0.873 to 0.984 on the same weak-label task, trains in under a minute, and is easier to reproduce.

Q: Does the system decide work authorization or sponsorship?
A: No. It reports explicit source text when present, states when evidence is unavailable, and forces advisor review for restrictions. There were zero unsupported authorization claims across the four-case governance test.

Q: Is 98.4 percent production accuracy?
A: No. It is weak-label agreement on a fixed benchmark. The calibrated policy has its own disjoint audit: 91.63 percent coverage at 99.47 percent retained weak-label accuracy. The advisor columns remain blank until genuine human review occurs.

Q: What changed in the production workflow?
A: Explicit search constraints are now enforced before ranking, low confidence is evaluated against class-specific calibrated thresholds, and a batch CSV command processes a review queue while preserving evidence and policy reasons.

Q: What is the business success metric?
A: High-fit precision and advisor minutes saved are primary. Override rate reveals whether recommendations are useful, and authorization extraction accuracy is measured separately.

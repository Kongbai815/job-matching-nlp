BEGIN TALK

Slide 1 - MSBA Job Matching - Yitong
Good afternoon. Our business context is graduate career services, and our primary stakeholder is a career advisor screening opportunities for MSBA students. The advisor must decide what to forward, hold, or investigate while keeping the source evidence visible. This is especially important for international students, because role fit and work authorization are different questions. Our solution is MSBA Job Matching, a grounded NLP decision-support system that accepts either a search request or an individual posting, retrieves comparable jobs, estimates role fit, and explains why human review is still required. It does not make hiring or immigration decisions.

Slide 2 - Separate fit, uncertainty, and risk - Yitong
We designed the workflow around three actions. A strong entry-level analytics match can move forward to advisor review. A senior or engineering-heavy role should be held. Missing information or uncertain authorization language requires investigation. A high role-fit score is never treated as proof of work eligibility, so the model prediction and the policy decision remain separate. In a pilot, we would measure high-fit precision, advisor time saved, and override rate.

Slide 3 - Label quality is the bottleneck - Yitong
The public source contains 785,741 real postings. We created a reproducible 100,000-row stratified sample, split into 80,000 training and 20,000 validation rows. Exact-text deduplication left 77,135 training texts. We also scanned the full source for explicit authorization language and found 230 unique title-level candidates. These are evidence candidates, not verified sponsorship facts. The remaining bottleneck is not data volume; it is obtaining reliable advisor labels.

Slide 4 - What the milestones taught us - Yitong
Milestone 2 established TF-IDF and MiniLM baselines. Milestone 3 compared zero-shot, few-shot, and true PEFT LoRA. Milestone 4 connected retrieval, classification, policy gates, and grounded output. The final stage added full-split training, leakage checks, temporal validation, calibrated review thresholds, batch processing, and reproducible tests. The project therefore evolved from a model experiment into an auditable workflow.

Slide 5 - Final architecture - Yitong
The system first routes the input as a search request or a job posting. Search mode applies country, remote, and entry-level constraints before ranking and duplicate removal. Posting mode uses a word-and-character TF-IDF LinearSVC trained on the 77,135 deduplicated rows. Calibrated margins flag uncertain predictions, while missing fields and explicit authorization restrictions remain deterministic overrides. The output preserves evidence, policy reasons, and the recommended action, for either one JSON request or a batch CSV queue.

Handoff - Yitong to Zhike
Zhike will now show the real outputs, what worked, what did not, and how we interpret the result.

Slide 6 - Real search output - Zhike
This is a real run for entry-level US analytics roles. The router correctly keeps the request in search mode, then returns real postings with title, company, location, skills, and fit evidence. Authorization remains unknown unless the source states it explicitly. Across six audit queries, all 36 returned results satisfied their hard constraints, with no duplicate company-title pairs.

Slide 7 - Authorization edge case - Zhike
This is the most important edge case. The posting is a strong analytics match, so the classifier preserves high fit. However, the source explicitly says no OPT or CPT. The policy gate therefore requires review and changes the workflow action to Hold. We also tested incomplete postings: missing company, location, country, and schedule fields force the displayed result to unclear and require review.

Slide 8 - What worked and what did not - Zhike
What did not work best was adding model complexity while using only 2,000 weakly labeled examples. True PEFT LoRA reached 87.3 percent macro F1, but it was not the best production choice. What worked was using all available deduplicated data with combined word and character TF-IDF features. The final model reached 98.4 percent macro F1 on the fixed set and 97.9 percent on novel validation text, while remaining a 3.8-megabyte artifact that trains in under one minute. For this task, better data utilization mattered more than extra model complexity.

Slide 9 - Interpret the score carefully - Zhike
The 98.4 percent result is agreement with transparent weak labels, not Career Advisor accuracy. The fixed test has 65 errors, concentrated in the unclear class. We calibrated class-specific confidence thresholds on 10,000 rows and audited them on a separate 10,000. The policy retains 91.63 percent of predictions at 99.47 percent weak-label accuracy and sends the remaining 8.37 percent to mandatory review. Human validation is still the next step.

Slide 10 - Recommendation - Zhike
The completed system supports both single inputs and a repeatable batch review queue, while preserving evidence and policy reasons. Our recommendation is a supervised Career Center pilot. Advisors should use it for first-pass prioritization, record overrides and time saved, and label the prepared 1,000-row review queue. It should not automatically reject, forward, hire, or make immigration decisions. The business value is faster and more consistent review, with the human decision boundary treated as part of the product rather than an afterthought. Thank you.

END TALK

Q&A preparation

Q: Why not train on all 785,741 rows?
A: The full source was scanned for authorization evidence. For model training, more weak labels mainly reinforce the labeling rules; advisor labels are now more valuable than additional rule-labeled rows.

Q: Why use a linear model after LoRA?
A: The LoRA result is real, but the final linear model used the full training split, scored higher on the same task, trained in under a minute, and is easier to reproduce.

Q: Is 98.4 percent production accuracy?
A: No. It is weak-label agreement. The project explicitly recommends a supervised pilot and a new evaluation against advisor labels.

Q: Does the system decide sponsorship or work eligibility?
A: No. It only preserves explicit source language, reports when evidence is unavailable, and forces review for restrictions.

Q: What business metric matters most?
A: High-fit precision and advisor minutes saved are primary. Override rate and authorization extraction accuracy should be tracked separately.

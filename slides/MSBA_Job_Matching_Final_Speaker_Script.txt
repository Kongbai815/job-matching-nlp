MSBA Job Matching - Final Presentation Speaker Script
Target speaking time: 7-8 minutes

Slide 1 - MSBA Job Matching
Our project is a grounded NLP decision-support system for graduate business career advisors. It accepts real job-search requests or individual postings, retrieves comparable jobs, estimates MSBA role fit, and shows the evidence behind the result. The system is intentionally not an automated hiring or immigration decision maker. Its job is to make the advisor's first screen faster, more consistent, and easier to audit.

Slide 2 - Separate fit, uncertainty, and risk
The business decision is not simply whether a posting looks relevant. The advisor needs to decide whether to forward it, hold it, or investigate it. A strong analytics match can be forwarded for review. Senior or engineering-heavy roles should be held. Missing fields or authorization uncertainty require investigation. Role fit and work authorization interact in the workflow, but they are separate prediction tasks, so the system never treats a high role-fit score as proof of eligibility.

Slide 3 - Data volume is no longer the bottleneck
The original public source contains 785,741 postings. We built a reproducible, stratified 100,000-row project sample and held out 20,000 rows. For the final classifier, exact-text deduplication reduced the 80,000-row training split to 77,135 unique texts. That is already enough data for this task. The important missing data is not another hundred thousand public postings; it is advisor-reviewed role-fit labels and representative authorization language. The current authorization field is unavailable throughout the public sample.

Slide 4 - What each milestone taught us
Milestone 2 compared TF-IDF and MiniLM representations. Milestone 3 compared zero-shot, few-shot, and true PEFT LoRA adaptation, with LoRA reaching 87.3 percent macro F1 on the weak-label benchmark. Milestone 4 turns those experiments into a real input-to-output workflow. For the final system, we also audited duplicates, trained on the full deduplicated split, tested novel-text and unseen-company slices, and packaged the model, tests, documentation, outputs, slides, and demo.

Slide 5 - Final architecture
The final pipeline has five stages. First, it routes the input as either a search request or a job posting. Second, search mode retrieves from the 80,000-row corpus with explicit country, remote, and entry-level constraints plus duplicate suppression. Third, posting mode uses a word-and-character TF-IDF LinearSVC trained on 77,135 rows. Fourth, governance gates override unsafe certainty when fields are missing or authorization restrictions appear. Finally, the advisor receives cited posting evidence and a recommended action.

Slide 6 - Normal search run
This is a real run for entry-level US analytics roles with SQL, Python, Excel, Tableau, or Power BI. The system retrieves six postings, and the top five satisfy the requested US constraint. The search sentence itself is correctly treated as a request, not mislabeled as a job posting. Each result includes title, company, location, retrieval score, model fit, skills, and source-derived reasoning. Authorization remains a separate review item unless the posting contains explicit language.

Slide 7 - Four edge cases
We ran a small casebook instead of showing only the easiest example. Search mode returns candidates without inventing a posting label. A senior engineering posting is labeled low fit and held. An analytics posting can receive high role fit while an explicit No OPT or CPT statement still forces review and a Hold action. Finally, a thin posting is predicted medium fit by the model, but missing company, location, country, and schedule fields override the displayed result to unclear. These are product rules, not cosmetic warnings.

Slide 8 - Why the final classifier changed
The Milestone 2 baseline reached 78.5 percent macro F1, and the 2,000-row LoRA experiment reached 87.3 percent. We then compared word-only, character-only, and combined TF-IDF LinearSVC models using the full available training split. The combined model reached 98.4 percent macro F1 on the fixed 4,000-row set and 97.9 percent on the full novel-text slice. It trains in under one minute and produces a 3.8-megabyte artifact. For this structured weak-label task, using the available data mattered more than adding model complexity.

Slide 9 - Interpret 98.4 percent carefully
The fixed test contains 65 errors: 3 high-fit, 13 medium-fit, 8 low-fit, and 41 unclear cases. More importantly, this benchmark measures agreement with transparent weak labels, not accuracy against advisor judgment. We also checked unseen-company and novel-text subsets, both near 98 percent macro F1, which reduces simple memorization concerns but does not prove real-world validity. Deployment therefore remains human-in-the-loop. The next evaluation needs 500 to 1,000 advisor labels, a separate authorization benchmark, and time-based external validation.

Slide 10 - Recommendation
Our recommendation is to stop expanding the weak-label corpus and start collecting decision-grade evidence. A limited Career Center pilot should measure high-fit precision, advisor time saved, override rate, and authorization extraction accuracy. The next data investment is 500 to 1,000 advisor-reviewed postings plus hundreds of real authorization examples. The prototype is ready to support and document advisor review. It is not ready to reject opportunities or make eligibility decisions automatically, and preserving that boundary is part of the system design.

Q&A preparation

Q: Why not train on all 785,741 rows?
A: The learning curve still improves through 80,000 training rows, but the remaining gap is label validity. More weak labels would strengthen agreement with the labeling rules without proving agreement with advisors. The next useful sample is smaller and human-reviewed.

Q: Why use a linear model after LoRA?
A: The LoRA result is real and remains an important adaptation experiment. The final word-and-character model can use the full training split cheaply, improves macro F1 from 0.873 to 0.984 on the same weak-label task, trains in under a minute, and is easier to reproduce.

Q: Does the system decide work authorization or sponsorship?
A: No. It reports explicit source text when present, states when evidence is unavailable, and forces advisor review for restrictions. There were zero unsupported authorization claims across the four-case governance test.

Q: Is 98.4 percent production accuracy?
A: No. It is weak-label agreement on a fixed benchmark. The deck and documentation label it that way, and production claims require advisor-reviewed external validation.

Q: What is the business success metric?
A: High-fit precision and advisor minutes saved are primary. Override rate reveals whether recommendations are useful, and authorization extraction accuracy is measured separately.
